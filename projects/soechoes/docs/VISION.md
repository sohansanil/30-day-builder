# SoEchoes — Project Vision

> Read this when you lose the thread.

---

## The Thesis

Every young person has at least one place they never forget.

A staircase where they sat after failing an exam.  
A rooftop where they spent nights wondering what to do with their life.  
A canteen table where they met their closest friends.  
A library corner where they realized who they wanted to become.

To everyone else, these places are ordinary.  
To us, they became part of who we are.

**SoEchoes is an anonymous, location-based memory archive that maps not places, but experiences.**

Its only purpose is to answer one question:

> **"Did anybody else feel this too?"**

---

## What SoEchoes Is

SoEchoes is:
- A digital archive of young adulthood
- A map of the places that changed us
- A machine for making strangers feel less alone
- A record of where we became who we are

SoEchoes is NOT:
- A social network
- A map application
- A confession board
- A content platform

---

## Why It's Called SoEchoes

**Echoes** because places hold echoes of the people who passed through them.  
The staircase still holds the sound of someone who cried there in 2019.  
The rooftop still holds the thoughts of someone who sat there at 2AM wondering about their future.

**So** because it's Sohan's project. Personal. Owned. Not generic.

---

## Why This Is Different From Everything Else

Most "anonymous sharing" apps fail because they try to be general.  
A general anonymous map collects:
- "I took the fattest dump here" (😂)
- "This is where I decided not to end my life" (💔)

In the same feed. That's emotional whiplash.

SoEchoes works because it has **emotional coherence**. The shared context is:

> Young adulthood. The years you were figuring yourself out.

Everyone who uses this shares that context. That's why it resonates.

---

## The Three Emotional Moments

The entire product is built around three feelings. Not features — feelings.

### 1. Discovery
*"Someone felt exactly what I felt."*  
This happens when a user clicks a pin and reads a memory that mirrors their own experience. The map pin is just a vessel. The feeling is the product.

### 2. Leaving Something Behind
*"I'm leaving something here forever."*  
This happens during the contribution ritual. Not a form submission — a ceremony. The user is contributing to something larger than themselves. Someone will find this years from now.

### 3. Realizing You Were Never Alone
*"Other people understand."*  
This happens when "I felt this too" counter ticks up on something the user wrote. They don't know who. But someone, somewhere, understood.

**Build those three feelings. Everything else is secondary.**

Before every feature, every design decision, every line of copy:
